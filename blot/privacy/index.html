InkBlot

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 27, 2026. Version 1.0 sent nothing at all; statistics arrived in 1.0.1, ad measurement in 1.1, the Studio in 1.2.

InkBlot is published by Bala Technologies LLC. It knows nothing about who you are. Your prints never leave your device unless you share them. The only things the app sends anywhere are anonymous statistics about how it is used and, to measure InkBlot's own ads, word to Google when the app is first opened, while it's in use, when a print is made and when the Studio is bought. One switch turns both off.

  • No accounts. There is no sign-in and no account.
  • No ads, and no tracking. InkBlot shows no ads. It never asks to track you, doesn't use your device's advertising identifier, and doesn't follow you across other apps or websites.
  • Anonymous usage statistics. To learn how InkBlot is used and make it better, the app sends anonymous statistics to PostHog, an analytics service: that the app was opened and for how long, which tools and inks were used, how a sheet was folded and opened, which screens were visited, and when the Studio was offered, bought or restored, and whether it's open. With them go a few facts about the device and the app: the device model, system version, screen size, language, whether it's on Wi-Fi or cellular, the app's name and version, whether it came from the App Store or TestFlight, a random number for each visit, and the version of PostHog's software in the app. Each installation is known only by a random number made up on the device. It isn't tied to your name, your Apple Account or anything else about you. Your prints, the names you give them and anything you type are never sent. Your location isn't collected: the app asks PostHog not to look up where the connection comes from, internet addresses are discarded, and the device's time zone is left out. You can turn the statistics off at any time in Settings, under Share anonymous usage statistics. That stops the recording at once: what was already recorded may still be sent, and after that the app doesn't contact PostHog at all.
  • Measuring InkBlot's ads. InkBlot is advertised on Google, and may be on other ad networks later. To learn which ads bring people who stay to make prints or buy the Studio, the app uses Google Analytics for Firebase, Google's measurement service: it tells Google that the app was opened for the first time, when it's in use, each time a print is made, and when the Studio is bought here, with its price and currency, and nothing else about what you do (nothing about the print: no picture, name or inks). Google's software adds the device model, system version, language and time zone, the app's version, and random numbers made up for this installation (not your device's advertising identifier). Google works out the country and region (such as the state) from the connection's internet address and doesn't keep the address. On the device, Google's software also checks whether the install followed one of InkBlot's ads, using short-lived signals such as ones derived from the internet address, without sending anything that identifies you. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland it makes no such check, and the app tells Google not to use this data in advertising, so there Google only estimates which ads worked. What we see are totals for each ad, such as how many installs, prints and purchases followed it, never a person, and none of it is used to show you ads. Separately, when an ad leads to an install, Apple tells the ad network, a day or more later and without saying whose device it was; InkBlot adds only how many prints have been made, up to five, and whether the Studio was bought. All of this follows the same switch as the statistics: turn off Share anonymous usage statistics in Settings and the app stops telling Google and Apple anything. What was already recorded may still be sent, and from the next launch the app doesn't contact Google at all.
  • Buying the Studio. The Studio is bought from Apple, through the App Store, and InkBlot never sees your payment details or your Apple Account. To know whether the Studio is yours, the app asks the App Store, on your device, whether this Apple Account owns it and which version of InkBlot it first downloaded: whoever paid for the app before it was free has the Studio without buying it. The statistics say whether it's open and why (bought, shared by your family, or had before the app was free), but never what was paid. Google is told the price of a purchase made on this device, and nothing about a Studio shared by your family or had before the app was free.
  • What stays on your device. Your prints, the inks you mix, your best scores, your settings, a count of the prints you've made and whether the Studio is open are kept in the app's own storage on your device. Deleting the app deletes them.
  • Motion. If you turn on Tilt, InkBlot reads which way the device is tilted so wet ink can run downhill. The readings aren't stored and never leave the device; the statistics say only whether Tilt was on.
  • The hinge. On iPhone Duo, InkBlot reads whether the phone is open or shut, to press the pages together. The readings stay on the device; the statistics say only that the device is an iPhone Duo and whether a sheet was folded or opened with the hinge.
  • Sharing. When you share a print, InkBlot hands the picture to the share sheet and you choose where it goes. InkBlot itself sends nothing. The words that go with it can carry a link to InkBlot on the App Store, marked so that Apple can tell us, in totals, how many downloads came from shared prints, never whose.
  • Children. InkBlot isn't directed at children, and the anonymous statistics are the same for everyone: they say nothing about who is using the app.
  • Changes. If this policy changes, the new version will appear in the app and on this page, with a new date.
