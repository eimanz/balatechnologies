InkBlot Duo

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 15, 2026. Version 1.0 sent nothing at all; statistics arrived in 1.0.1, ad measurement in 1.0.3.

InkBlot Duo is published by Bala Technologies LLC. It knows nothing about who you are. Your prints never leave your device unless you share them. The only things the app sends anywhere are anonymous statistics about how it is used and, to measure InkBlot Duo's own ads, word to Google when the app is first opened and when a print is made. One switch turns both off.

  • No accounts. There is no sign-in and no account.
  • No ads, and no tracking. InkBlot Duo shows no ads. It never asks to track you, doesn't use your device's advertising identifier, and doesn't follow you across other apps or websites.
  • Anonymous usage statistics. To learn how InkBlot Duo is used and make it better, the app sends anonymous statistics to PostHog, an analytics service: that the app was opened and for how long, which tools and inks were used, how a sheet was folded and opened, and which screens were visited. With them go a few facts about the device and the app: the device model, system version, screen size, language, whether it's on Wi-Fi or cellular, the app's name and version, whether it came from the App Store or TestFlight, a random number for each visit, and the version of PostHog's software in the app. Each installation is known only by a random number made up on the device. It isn't tied to your name, your Apple Account or anything else about you. Your prints, the names you give them and anything you type are never sent. Your location isn't collected: the app asks PostHog not to look up where the connection comes from, internet addresses are discarded, and the device's time zone is left out. You can turn the statistics off at any time in Settings, under Share anonymous usage statistics. That stops the recording at once: what was already recorded may still be sent, and after that the app doesn't contact PostHog at all.
  • Measuring InkBlot Duo's ads. InkBlot Duo is advertised on Google, and may be on other ad networks later. To learn which ads bring people who stay to make prints, the app uses Google Analytics for Firebase, Google's measurement service: it tells Google that the app was opened for the first time, when it's in use, and each time a print is made, and nothing else about what you do (nothing about the print: no picture, name or inks). Google's software adds the device model, system version and language, the app's version, and a random number made up for this installation (not your device's advertising identifier). Google works out the country and region (such as the state) from the connection's internet address and doesn't keep the address. On the device, Google's software also checks whether the install followed one of InkBlot Duo's ads, using short-lived signals such as ones derived from the internet address, without sending anything that identifies you. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland it makes no such check, and the app tells Google not to use this data for advertising. What we see are totals for each ad, such as how many installs and prints followed it, never a person, and none of it is used to show you ads. Separately, when an ad leads to an install, Apple tells the ad network, a day or more later and without saying whose device it was; InkBlot Duo adds only how many prints have been made, up to five. All of this follows the same switch as the statistics: turn off Share anonymous usage statistics in Settings and the app stops telling Google and Apple anything. What was already recorded may still be sent, and from the next launch the app doesn't contact Google at all.
  • What stays on your device. Your prints, the inks you mix, your best scores, your settings and a count of the prints you've made are kept in the app's own storage on your device. Deleting the app deletes them.
  • Motion. If you turn on Tilt, InkBlot Duo reads which way the device is tilted so wet ink can run downhill. The readings aren't stored and never leave the device; the statistics say only whether Tilt was on.
  • The hinge. On iPhone Duo, InkBlot Duo reads whether the phone is open or shut, to press the pages together. The readings stay on the device; the statistics say only that the device is an iPhone Duo and whether a sheet was folded or opened with the hinge.
  • Sharing. When you share a print, InkBlot Duo hands the picture to the share sheet and you choose where it goes. InkBlot Duo itself sends nothing.
  • Children. InkBlot Duo isn't directed at children, and the anonymous statistics are the same for everyone: they say nothing about who is using the app.
  • Changes. If this policy changes, the new version will appear in the app and on this page, with a new date.
