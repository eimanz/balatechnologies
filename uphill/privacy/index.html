Uphill

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 15, 2026. Ad measurement arrived in version 1.2.

Uphill: Roll Ball Arcade Game (“Uphill”) is a game published by Bala Technologies LLC. It has no accounts and shows no ads, and it doesn't sell information about you or use it to show you ads. The only things it sends anywhere are anonymous statistics about how it is played, a report if it crashes, and, to measure Uphill's own ads, word to Google when the app is first opened, while it's in use, and when a game is finished. One switch turns all of it off.

  • No accounts. There is no sign-in and no account.
  • No ads, and no tracking. Uphill shows no ads. It never asks to track you, doesn't use your device's advertising identifier, and doesn't follow you across other apps or websites.
  • Anonymous usage statistics. To learn how Uphill is played and make it better, the app sends anonymous statistics to PostHog, an analytics service: that the app was opened and for how long, which of the game's two tables was played, how each ball was rolled (a flick, a hold or a tap, how hard, and the slope it was rolled at), where it ended up and what it scored, each game's score, which screens were visited and which settings were changed. A few technical details go with them: the device model, system version, screen size and language, whether it's on Wi-Fi or cellular, the app's name and version, whether it came from the App Store or TestFlight, a random number for each visit, and the version of PostHog's software in the app. Each installation is known only by a random number made up on the device. It isn't tied to your name, your Apple Account or anything else about you. Your location isn't collected: the app asks PostHog not to look up where the connection comes from, internet addresses are discarded, and the device's time zone is left out. You can turn the statistics off at any time in Settings, under Share anonymous usage statistics. That stops the recording at once: what was already recorded may still be sent, and after that the app doesn't contact PostHog at all.
  • Measuring Uphill's ads. Uphill is advertised on Google, and may be on other ad networks later. To learn which ads bring players who stay to play, the app uses Google Analytics for Firebase, Google's measurement service: it tells Google that the app was opened for the first time, when it's in use, and each time a game is finished, and nothing else about the game (no scores, slopes or settings). Google's software adds the device model, system version, language and time zone, the app's version, and random numbers made up for this installation (not your device's advertising identifier). Google works out the country and region (such as the state) from the connection's internet address and doesn't keep the address. On the device, Google's software also checks whether the install followed one of Uphill's ads, using short-lived signals such as ones derived from the internet address, without sending anything that identifies you. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland it makes no such check, and the app tells Google not to use this data in advertising, so there Google only estimates which ads worked. What we see are totals for each ad, such as how many installs and finished games followed it, never a person, and none of it is used to show you ads. Separately, when an ad leads to an install, Apple tells the ad network, a day or more later and without saying whose device it was; Uphill adds only how many games have been finished, up to five. All of this follows the same switch as the statistics: turn off Share anonymous usage statistics in Settings and the app stops telling Google and Apple anything. What was already recorded may still be sent, and from the next launch the app doesn't contact Google at all.
  • Crash reports. If Uphill crashes or freezes, it sends a report to Sentry, a crash reporting service, so the problem can be found and fixed: what the app was doing when it failed, the app's version, the device model and system version, and the device's memory and storage at the time. Reports carry no account, name, internet address, time zone or anything else that says who or where you are, and no screenshots or record of what you tapped. They follow the same switch as the statistics: turn off Share anonymous usage statistics in Settings and no more reports are sent.
  • What stays on your device. Your best score, your settings and a count of the games you've finished are kept in the app's own storage on your device. Deleting the app deletes them, and you can reset your best score in Settings.
  • The hinge. On iPhone Duo, Uphill reads the angle of the hinge to set the slope of the ramp while you play. It also remembers what the angle reads when the phone is fully open, so the slope is right the next time. The slope each ball was rolled at, whether the hinge or the slider set it, and how the phone was folded (like a laptop, flat, or like a book) are part of the anonymous statistics. Nothing else about the hinge leaves the device.
  • Children. Uphill isn't directed at children, and the anonymous statistics are the same for everyone.
  • Changes. If this policy changes, the new version will appear in the app and on this page, with a new date.
